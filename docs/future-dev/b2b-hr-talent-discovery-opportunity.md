# Aptitude Search: B2B HR / Talent Discovery Opportunity

## Initial Observation: AARP Career Site Validation

The AARP career site uses something very similar, in concept, to Aptitude Search:

1. Candidate submits resume.
2. Site analyzes resume.
3. Site returns jobs related to what appears in the submitted resume.

The major difference is scope:

- AARP operates over a smaller, controlled corpus:
  - AARP jobs
  - AARP career opportunities
  - A defined set of roles

- Aptitude Search attempts to operate over a much larger corpus:
  - potentially the entire job market
  - broader company/job discovery
  - identifying opportunities that keyword matching would miss

This provides some external validation that the basic workflow is valuable:
- resume understanding
- candidate profiling
- semantic job matching

The difference is that Aptitude Search aims to make the matching layer more general and capability-oriented.

# Potential Shift: Sell Aptitude Search to Companies / Organizations

A stronger business direction may be positioning Aptitude Search as an HR / hiring tool rather than only a job seeker tool.

The candidate-side version:

> Resume → Aptitude Profile → Career directions + matching opportunities

The employer-side version:

> Resume database → Aptitude Profiles → Better candidate-role matching

Companies have the inverse problem of job seekers:

Candidates ask:
> "What jobs am I actually suited for?"

Companies ask:
> "Which candidates are actually suited for this role?"

Current recruiting systems often rely heavily on:
- keywords
- job titles
- exact resume terminology
- ATS filters

This creates problems because candidates may have the right capabilities without using the expected words.

Example:

Candidate:
> "Built distributed backend services"

Job description:
> "Cloud-native API development"

Keyword matching may miss the relationship.

Semantic understanding should recognize the connection.

# Employer Value Proposition

Aptitude Search could be positioned as:

> "A semantic talent discovery engine that understands candidate capabilities beyond keywords."

Potential benefits:

- Find overlooked candidates.
- Reduce dependence on exact resume wording.
- Discover alternative career paths for candidates.
- Improve internal mobility.
- Better match applicants to roles.
- Surface hidden talent.

---

# The Internal Mobility Opportunity

The strongest B2B use case may not be external hiring.

It may be helping large organizations understand their existing workforce.

Example:

Employee profile:
- 5 years Java experience
- Python automation projects
- internal tooling experience
- interest in AI

Current role:
- Business analyst

Aptitude Search could identify:

> "This employee has strong signals for AI automation engineering, developer productivity, or technical product roles."

Large organizations often have thousands of employees whose skills are poorly understood.

This creates a major opportunity.

---

# Why This Fits Aptitude Search

The core thesis remains the same:

Traditional systems match based on labels.

Aptitude Search matches based on meaning.

The product is not just:

> "Find jobs containing these keywords."

It is:

> "Understand what this person can do and where that capability is valuable."

This applies equally to:

- job seekers
- recruiters
- hiring managers
- internal mobility teams
- workforce planning

# Competitive Landscape

The idea is not completely unexplored.

Recruiting technology companies already use AI for:
- candidate matching
- resume parsing
- talent search
- ranking applicants

However, the differentiation could be:

Existing recruiting platforms:
> "Help recruiters search candidates."

Aptitude Search:
> "Build a deeper understanding of candidate capability."

The opportunity is not inventing AI recruiting from scratch.

The opportunity is creating a capability intelligence layer.

# Strategic Takeaway

The AARP example suggests that the underlying idea behind Aptitude Search has real-world validation.

The strongest commercial opportunity may not be competing directly with job boards.

A better path may be:

1. Build the candidate capability intelligence engine.
2. Apply it to company hiring systems.
3. Expand into internal talent discovery and workforce intelligence.

The fundamental insight:

> The job market is currently optimized for matching words. Aptitude Search is trying to match capabilities.
